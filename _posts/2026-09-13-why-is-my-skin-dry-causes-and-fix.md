If your skin feels tight, rough, or flaky — especially after cleansing — the underlying cause is almost always the same thing: your **skin barrier** isn't holding onto moisture the way it should.

## What the skin barrier actually does

The outermost layer of your skin (the *stratum corneum*) is often compared to a brick wall — skin cells are the "bricks," and lipids like ceramides, cholesterol, and fatty acids are the "mortar" holding everything together. When that mortar is intact, water stays in and irritants stay out. When it's compromised, water escapes faster than your skin can replace it, and you end up with the classic signs of dryness: tightness, flaking, rough texture, sometimes redness or stinging.

## Common causes of dry skin

- **Over-cleansing or harsh cleansers.** Foaming cleansers with sulfates strip natural oils along with dirt.
- **Hot showers.** Hot water breaks down the lipid barrier faster than lukewarm water.
- **Over-exfoliating.** Frequent use of AHAs, BHAs, or physical scrubs thins the barrier faster than it can rebuild.
- **Low humidity.** Cold, dry air (common in winter or heavy AC) pulls moisture out of skin.
- **Layering too many actives at once.** Retinoids, vitamin C, and exfoliants stacked together can overwhelm skin that hasn't built up tolerance.
- **Skin conditions.** Eczema and psoriasis involve genuine barrier dysfunction, not just surface dryness — see a dermatologist if dryness is severe, persistent, or accompanied by intense itching.

## How to fix dry skin, step by step

1. **Simplify your routine.** Drop exfoliants and strong actives temporarily — give your skin a break while it repairs.
2. **Switch to a gentle, low-pH cleanser.** Cream or gel cleansers that don't foam aggressively clean without stripping.
3. **Use lukewarm water**, not hot, when washing your face.
4. **Apply moisturizer to damp skin.** This helps trap water at the surface rather than letting it evaporate.
5. **Look for barrier-repairing ingredients**: ceramides, squalane, cholesterol, fatty acids, and glycerin are the ones actually shown to help rebuild the lipid layer — not just sit on top of skin.
6. **Add an occlusive at night if needed.** A thin layer of something like petrolatum or a balm seals moisture in overnight for more stubborn dryness.
7. **Don't skip sunscreen.** Sun damage itself weakens the barrier over time — daily SPF protects the repair work you're doing.

## Best ingredients to look for (and what to avoid)

| Look for | Why | Avoid (for now) |
|---|---|---|
| Ceramides | Rebuild the lipid barrier directly | Alcohol-heavy toners |
| Hyaluronic acid | Draws water into skin | High-strength retinoids |
| Squalane | Lightweight, mimics skin's natural oils | Physical scrubs |
| Glycerin | Reliable humectant, works in any routine | Fragrance in already-irritated skin |

## The bottom line

Dry skin is rarely about needing "more moisture" in a vague sense — it's usually about repairing a barrier that's being disrupted faster than it can heal. Strip the routine back, prioritize barrier-repair ingredients, and give it a few weeks before judging whether something is working.

*This article is for general information and isn't a substitute for advice from a dermatologist, especially for persistent or severe skin conditions.*
